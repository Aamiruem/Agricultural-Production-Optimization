# Soil testing and N-P-K

## Why test soil
A soil test tells you how much nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) is already available, plus pH, organic carbon and micronutrients. In India the Soil Health Card scheme provides free tests and crop-wise fertilizer recommendations. Test every two to three years, and sample before sowing rather than right after fertilizing.

## How to take a soil sample
Collect 10 to 15 small cores from a zig-zag path across one uniform field, at plough depth (0 to 15 cm). Avoid bunds, fertilizer bands, compost heaps and wet spots. Mix the cores in a clean bucket, remove stones and roots, and send about 500 g in a labelled cloth bag to the soil testing laboratory.

## Role of nitrogen
Nitrogen drives leaf and stem growth and gives plants their green colour. Deficiency shows as pale yellowing that starts on older, lower leaves and stunted growth. Too much nitrogen causes soft, lodging-prone growth, more pest attack and delayed maturity, and the excess leaches into groundwater.

## Role of phosphorus
Phosphorus supports root development, flowering and seed formation, and is most needed early in the season. Deficiency causes poor rooting, delayed maturity and sometimes purple or dull dark-green leaves. Phosphorus moves very little in soil, so it should be placed near the seed at sowing.

## Role of potassium
Potassium improves water regulation, disease resistance, grain filling and fruit quality. Deficiency shows as scorching or browning along the margins of older leaves and weak stalks. Fruit crops such as banana, grapes and apple have high potassium demand.
