# Common fertilizers in India

## Nutrient content of common fertilizers
Urea contains 46% nitrogen. DAP (di-ammonium phosphate, grade 18-46-0) contains 18% nitrogen and 46% phosphate (P2O5). MOP (muriate of potash) contains 60% potash (K2O). SSP (single super phosphate) contains 16% P2O5 and also supplies sulphur and calcium. NPK complexes such as 10-26-26 and 12-32-16 supply all three nutrients together.

## Calculating a dose
To supply a nutrient, divide the required kilograms of nutrient by the fertilizer's nutrient fraction. For example, 50 kg of nitrogen needs about 50 / 0.46 = 109 kg of urea. When DAP is used for phosphorus, subtract the nitrogen it supplies (18% of the DAP weight) before calculating the urea dose.

## Timing and splitting nitrogen
Apply phosphorus and potassium as a basal dose at sowing. Split nitrogen into two or three doses, for example at sowing, at tillering or vegetative growth, and before flowering, because a single large dose is easily lost by leaching and volatilisation. Neem-coated urea releases nitrogen more slowly and reduces losses.

## Balanced and integrated nutrient management
Combining fertilizers with farmyard manure, compost or green manure improves soil organic matter and nutrient-use efficiency. Pulses fix atmospheric nitrogen with Rhizobium bacteria and usually need only a small starter dose of nitrogen. Excessive use of urea alone, without phosphorus and potassium, degrades soil health over time.
