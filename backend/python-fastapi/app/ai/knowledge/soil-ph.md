# Soil pH management

## What pH means for crops
Soil pH measures acidity on a 0 to 14 scale; 7 is neutral. Most field crops grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.5, where nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are most available. Outside this range nutrients become locked up even when the soil test shows them present.

## Correcting acidic soils
Acidic soils (pH below about 5.5) are common in high-rainfall regions such as the North-East, Kerala and parts of Odisha. They are corrected by applying agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) or dolomite, based on a lime-requirement test, preferably a few weeks before sowing. Crops such as tea, pineapple and potato tolerate acidity better than pulses.

## Correcting alkaline and sodic soils
Alkaline soils (pH above about 8.0) and sodic soils with high exchangeable sodium occur in parts of Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan. Sodic soils are reclaimed with gypsum followed by leaching with good-quality water, along with organic matter. Tolerant crops include barley, cotton and some rice varieties.
