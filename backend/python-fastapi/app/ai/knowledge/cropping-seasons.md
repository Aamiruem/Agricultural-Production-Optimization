# Indian cropping seasons

## Kharif season
Kharif crops are sown with the onset of the south-west monsoon, roughly June to July, and harvested from September to October. They need warm, wet conditions. Typical kharif crops are rice, maize, cotton, jute, pigeon pea, moth bean, black gram and groundnut.

## Rabi season
Rabi crops are sown after the monsoon, roughly October to December, and harvested from March to April. They grow in cool weather on residual soil moisture or irrigation and need warm, dry weather at ripening. Typical rabi crops are wheat, chickpea, lentil, mustard, barley and peas.

## Zaid season
Zaid is the short summer season between rabi and kharif, roughly March to June, and depends on irrigation. Typical zaid crops are watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables and mung bean.

## Crop rotation
Rotating cereals with pulses, for example rice followed by chickpea or lentil, adds nitrogen to the soil, breaks pest and disease cycles and improves yields of the following crop. Avoid growing the same crop on the same field season after season.
